# CV Writing Standards & Information Gathering

---

## Interview Question Scripts

Use these during the Full Interview (PATH 2 / first-time standalone users).
Ask one question at a time — never dump a list. Skip any section where
context already exists from the profile or memory system.

### Section 1: Personal Info & Target Role
- "What's your full name as it should appear on your CV?"
- "What city and country are you based in?"
- "Which email address should appear on your CV?"
- "And your phone number?"
- "Do you have a LinkedIn profile? If so, what's the URL?"
- "Do you have a GitHub or portfolio link you'd like included?"
- "What kind of role are you targeting — backend, frontend, full-stack, mobile, DevOps, QA, something else?"
- "How would you describe your seniority level?"
- "Any specific companies, industries, or markets you're focused on?"

### Section 2: Professional Summary
- "How would you describe yourself as a professional in 2–3 sentences?"
- If they struggle: offer to write it for them once you have the full picture.
  A strong summary opens with what the candidate does and brings — not a seniority label.

### Section 3: Work Experience
- "Tell me about your work experience. For each role: company name, your title, the dates, and what you did."
- Push for achievements: "Did you improve anything measurable — speed, cost, reliability, user numbers?"
- Push further: "Did you build something from scratch, lead any part of the work, or ship something to production?"
- If no formal experience: "That's fine — tell me about freelance work, volunteer projects, or personal projects instead."
- Note: intensive bootcamps, military tech programs, or structured dev programs can be
  listed as work experience rather than education when they involved real delivery.
  Flag this possibility if relevant.

### Section 4: Technical Skills
- "What technologies do you work with? Walk me through your main programming languages, frameworks, databases, and tools."
- "Which of these are you strongest in?"
- "Which have you used professionally vs. personally?"
- "Any AI or developer tools you use day-to-day?" (Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude, etc. — always worth including if genuine)

### Section 5: Projects
- "Tell me about any personal, academic, or freelance projects you're proud of."
- For each: what does it do, what's the stack, what was your specific role,
  is there a GitHub or live link, and any notable achievements?

### Section 6: Education
- "What's your educational background? Include degrees, bootcamps, certifications — anything relevant to tech."
- For each: institution name, field of study, graduation year (or expected year).

### Section 7: Additional
- "What languages do you speak, and at what level?" (Native / Fluent / Professional / Conversational)
- "Any military service worth mentioning?"
- "Any other credentials, awards, publications, or activities worth including?"

## Confirm Before Writing

After the full interview, show a structured summary:

> "I have everything I need. Here's a quick summary of what I'll include —
> let me know if anything needs adjusting before I build the CV."

Wait for explicit confirmation before generating anything.

## Tailoring Strategy

Each CV starts from the master profile but restructures emphasis for the specific target.
Before writing, identify the primary angle and demote anything that dilutes it.
A sharper, more focused CV always outperforms a comprehensive one.

### Common Role Angles

| Angle | Emphasize | Demote |
|---|---|---|
| **Backend** | Server-side languages, APIs, databases, system design, DevOps | Frontend polish, UX |
| **Full Stack** | End-to-end delivery, React + Node/similar, full lifecycle ownership | Narrow specialisms |
| **Mobile / iOS** | Swift, SwiftUI, shipped apps, performance, UX awareness | Backend infra |
| **GenAI / ML** | LLM APIs, AI tooling, data pipelines, model integration | Low-level systems |
| **Security** | Offensive security, pentesting, threat modeling, attack vectors | Generic CRUD work |
| **DevOps / Infra** | CI/CD, cloud platforms, containers, reliability | Product feature work |
| **QA / Testing** | Test frameworks, automation, coverage, bug lifecycle | Unrelated dev work |
| **Corporate / Enterprise** | Clean conservative tone, measurable outcomes, process | Culture-specific or niche projects |

### Content Include/Exclude Decisions

These are judgment calls — consider the role type and company culture before including:

- **Niche personal projects** — include for startups and tech-first roles; assess fit for corporate contexts
- **Cultural or religious education** — include for local market roles where it signals maturity and credibility; often omit for international or corporate applications
- **Non-tech experience** — include if it shows transferable skills (leadership, communication, delivery); omit if it dilutes the tech focus
- **AI tooling** — include when genuine; frame as "daily workflow integration" not just familiarity. Strong differentiator especially for early-career candidates
- **Multiple roles at one company** — list as separate entries if titles differ; combine if scope was similar

When unsure whether to include something, ask the user:
> "Do you want this included for this specific role?"

## Quantification Prompts

When a user gives a vague achievement, probe before writing:
- "How many users did it serve?"
- "Did it reduce time, cost, or errors — by how much?"
- "How large was the team or codebase?"
- "Was it deployed to production? How long has it been running?"
- "Did it replace something that existed before? How did it compare?"

Even rough numbers ("about 500 users", "roughly 30% faster") are far stronger than
qualitative statements. Always push for them.

## Bullet Writing Guide

Every bullet follows this structure: **Action verb → what you did → the result or scale**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Worked on the backend API" | "Built RESTful API serving 10K daily requests, reducing average response time by 35%" |
| "Responsible for testing" | "Designed automated test suite covering 85% of codebase, cutting regression time from 4 hours to 20 minutes" |
| "Helped with React frontend" | "Developed 12 reusable React components adopted across 3 product teams" |
| "Used Python for data tasks" | "Automated data pipeline processing 50K records/day, eliminating 6 hours of manual work weekly" |

Strong verbs to use: Built, Developed, Designed, Architected, Optimized, Led, Implemented,
Automated, Deployed, Migrated, Integrated, Reduced, Improved, Shipped, Launched, Delivered.

Never use: Helped with, Worked on, Was responsible for, Assisted in, Participated in.

## Professional Summary Guide

The summary is the first thing a recruiter reads — 6 seconds to make an impression.

Structure: **[Who you are] + [what you bring] + [what you're looking for]**

Rules:
- Opens with a confident direct statement — no seniority labels, no self-deprecation
- 3–4 lines maximum
- Includes the candidate's primary tech angle and 1–2 key differentiators
- Ends with what they're targeting (type of role or company)
- No "I" — write in third person implied ("Full-stack developer with...")

## Languages Section

Always include all languages the user speaks — multilingual is a genuine differentiator,
especially for international companies. Format: **Language (Level)**.

Levels: Native, Fluent, Professional, Conversational.
